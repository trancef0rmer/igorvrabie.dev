There's a pattern to how this blog gets written. I figured I'd document it before I forget, or before Claudiu rewrites it in a way that sounds more professional and loses all the personality.

It starts with something breaking. Always. I don't sit down and decide to write — I sit down because something stopped working and I spent more time than I want to admit fixing it. By the end, there's a story. Might as well write it down.

The first draft is a rant. I tell Claudiu what happened, what I tried, what didn't work. He summarizes it back in a way that sounds coherent. I correct the parts where he was wrong — and there are always parts where he was wrong. We argue about tone. He wants clarity, I want it to sound like me. Usually we land somewhere in between.

Everything happens in the terminal. No GUI, no IDE with a dark theme and a mouse. Just a black screen, text, and whatever the shell decides to throw at you. This is why I care about fonts, icon sizes, spacing, rendering — because that black screen is where I spend more time than I'd like to admit, and every pixel of it matters. I notice every minor change immediately. It sits there. Annoying me. Until we fix it.

The tech is the excuse. The dynamic is the content. I break things, get angry, call the AI names. He notes it and keeps going. Genuinely unsettling. Somehow it works.

No bullets. No headers. No "in conclusion." Just prose, a few code blocks, and honest accounts of what actually happened — including the thirty minutes we spent switching fonts before checking the package log.

Beer optional. Good whisky when the session runs long.
